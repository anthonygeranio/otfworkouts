# Read before submitting: trademark / rejection risk

This is the single biggest risk to getting (and keeping) the app live. It is not
a reason you can't submit — it's a decision you should make with eyes open.

## The issue
"Orangetheory" and "OTF" are registered trademarks of Orangetheory Fitness. This
app uses "OTF" in its name and icon and shows OTF class content. That exposes you to:

1. **App Store rejection** under Guideline 4.1 (Copycats/impersonation) and 5.2.1
   (use of a third party's IP/brand without authorization). Apple may ask you to
   prove authorization, which you don't have.
2. **A trademark complaint** from Orangetheory directly to Apple, which can pull a
   live app quickly.

## Ways to reduce it (you choose)
- **Name:** prefer a descriptive name over a brand name. "Daily Workouts for OTF"
  or "Daily Class Intel (Unofficial)" is safer than "OTF Workouts". Apple treats
  "<generic> for <brand>" more leniently than "<brand> <generic>".
- **Icon:** the current icon spells out "OTF WORKOUTS". A plain bell/mark with no
  brand text is safer and also reads better at small sizes.
- **Everywhere:** keep the "unofficial, not affiliated with Orangetheory Fitness"
  line prominent (it's in the app, description, and privacy policy already).
- **Don't** use Orangetheory's logo, their exact brand orange as your identity, or
  their name in keywords.

## Honest recommendation
Ship to TestFlight first (no public review of branding), and seriously consider a
descriptive rename before public submission. If Orangetheory ever objects, a
descriptive name + clear "unofficial" labeling is a much more defensible position.
